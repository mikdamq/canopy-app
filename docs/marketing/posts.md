### English

Most vertical farms use about 10–18 kWh of electricity for every kilo of lettuce.

That's what a 2025 review of 16 published studies found. The same review estimates a well-run farm could get to about 3–7 kWh per kg.

The gap is mostly light and cooling. And you can't close a gap you don't measure.

## Post 7: what a digital twin is

Visual: the tower in daylight, overview, with three short labels pinned to it ("your layout", "your readings", "your crops").

### English

"Digital twin" sounds like something only big factories can afford.

It isn't. A farm digital twin is a live copy of your farm on screen: the same rooms, racks, floors and lights, drawn in 3D and kept up to date with your data.

Three things people get wrong:
• It's not a generic dashboard. It's built on your real layout.
• You don't need sensors to start. A spreadsheet, or readings taken by hand, is enough.
• It helps most indoors, where many floors or containers are slow to check on foot.

## Post 8: water

Visual: two bars, 250 L and 20 L, with a lettuce icon; source line at the bottom.

### English

Field lettuce: about 250 litres of water per kilo.
Hydroponic greenhouse lettuce: about 20.

That's from a well-known 2015 study (Barbosa et al.). In Jordan and the Gulf, where every litre counts, that gap matters.

But the same study found the hydroponic farm needed far more energy per kilo. Water saved, power spent.

So the honest answer to "does hydroponics save water?" is: yes, a lot. And you should track the energy it costs you, floor by floor, at the same time.

## Post 9: Gulf heat

Visual: the tower at night with the LEDs on, the clock reading 23:00.

### English

In a closed indoor farm, most of the heat your cooling fights comes from inside: the lights and the plants. In Jordan and the Gulf, the heat outside comes on top.

One simple move helps: run the lights at night, when the outside air is cooler.

In a 2023 study of container farms in different climates, that cut cooling energy by up to 19.4%.

The same study found hotter climates use more energy, and that energy-saving tricks work less well there. So in the Gulf, the basics matter more: insulation, night lighting, and watching humidity as closely as temperature.

👉 https://laminafarm.app/en/learn/gulf-heat-indoor-farming?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post9

#IndoorFarming #ContainerFarming #Energy #UAE #SaudiArabia

في المزرعة الداخلية المغلقة، يأتي معظم الحرارة التي يقاومها التبريد من الداخل: من الإضاءة ومن النباتات نفسها. وفي الأردن والخليج تُضاف حرارة الخارج فوق ذلك.

## Post 10: cost per kg

Visual: a single line of type: "Cost per kg = monthly running costs ÷ kilos sold".

### English

One number decides whether your farm pays: cost per kilo.

Cost per kg = everything it cost to run the farm this month ÷ the kilos you sold this month.

Two rules make it honest:
• Count everything: electricity, labour, seeds and nutrients, packaging, water, rent, repairs.
• Use kilos sold, not harvested. Waste is a cost, not output.

Then compare it with your selling price. If cost is higher, the farm loses money on every kilo, however good the crop looks.

Work it out monthly, and per floor or container if you can. That's where you find which part of the farm pays.

👉 https://laminafarm.app/en/learn/cost-per-kg?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post10

#FarmManagement #VerticalFarming #AgTech #Startups

### العربية

رقم واحد يحدّد إن كانت مزرعتك رابحة: تكلفة الكيلوغرام.

تكلفة الكيلوغرام = كل ما كلّفه تشغيل المزرعة هذا الشهر ÷ الكيلوغرامات التي بعتها هذا الشهر.

قاعدتان تجعلانه رقمًا صادقًا:
• احسب كل شيء: الكهرباء والأجور والبذور والمغذيات والتغليف والماء والإيجار والصيانة.
• احسب ما بعته لا ما حصدته؛ فالهدر تكلفة وليس إنتاجًا.

ثم قارنه بسعر البيع. إن كانت التكلفة أعلى، فالمزرعة تخسر في كل كيلوغرام مهما بدا المحصول جيدًا.

احسبه كل شهر، ولكل طابق أو حاوية إن استطعت؛ فهناك تعرف أي جزء من المزرعة يربح.

## Post 11: seed to delivery in five moments

Visual: a carousel (LinkedIn document, 6 slides): one slide per moment, then "one batch ID". Uses the demo's seed-to-plate strip.

### English

Can you trace any box you delivered back to the tray it was seeded in?

You can, with one habit: give every batch one ID when it's seeded, and record five moments against it.

1. Seed
2. Move to its growing spot
3. Harvest
4. Pack
5. Deliver

Add where it grew (floor, rack or container), and the climate and light it grew under belong to that batch too.

Start with a spreadsheet. Move to a tool when keeping it up takes too long.

👉 https://laminafarm.app/en/learn/seed-to-delivery-tracking?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=post11

## Post 13: light recipes and DLI

Visual: the formula card, then the demo's light-recipe panel.

### English

How much light does lettuce actually need in a day?

Growers measure it as DLI, the daily light integral:
DLI = PPFD × hours of light × 3,600 ÷ 1,000,000

So 200 µmol/m²/s for 16 hours gives about 11.5 mol/m²/day.

A 2023 study found 11.5 mol/m²/day was the best DLI for iceberg lettuce in an indoor vertical system.

The catch: light intensity drives both growth and your electricity bill (and cooling with it). More light is not always better. The right recipe is the one that pays.

كم يحتاج الخس من الضوء في اليوم فعلًا؟

يقيسه المزارعون بالتكامل الضوئي اليومي (DLI):

## Post 14: why big farms failed

### English

Between 2022 and 2025, several of the best-funded indoor farms in the US and Europe closed or went through bankruptcy.

The reasons reported most often: high energy costs, tight funding, and growing big before each farm paid its way. Put simply, it cost more to grow a kilo than buyers would pay for it.

A small farm can avoid the same trap by watching five numbers every month, from the first harvest:
• Cost per kg
• Energy per kg
• Yield per floor or container
• Waste
• How full your racks are

None of these need a big team. They need a habit.

## Post 15: yield per square metre

Visual: the same farm drawn twice: "growing area (all levels)" vs "building footprint".

### English

"We grow X kilos per square metre." Per which square metre?

Yield per m² = sellable kilos ÷ growing area ÷ time. Three choices change the answer:

• Which area: growing area across all levels, or the building's footprint. In a vertical farm they give very different numbers.
• Which kilos: sold, not harvested.
• Which time: per year, so crops with different cycles compare fairly.

Pick a method, write it down, and use it every month for every floor and container. Then the number means something.

### العربية

"ننتج كذا كيلوغرامًا لكل متر مربع". لكل أي متر مربع؟

الإنتاج لكل م² = الكيلوغرامات القابلة للبيع ÷ مساحة الزراعة ÷ المدة. وثلاثة اختيارات تغيّر الجواب:

### English

Container farm or vertical farm: what should you track?

The same basics in both: climate, water and nutrients, light, batches and energy.

The difference is where you look:
• Container farms: watch each container on its own. Small spaces change fast.
• Vertical farms: watch the differences between floors and zones. Warm air rises, and airflow is rarely even.

Start with climate and batches; add light and energy next.
